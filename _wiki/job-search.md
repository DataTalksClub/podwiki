Job search is the candidate-side work of choosing a data, analytics, ML, or AI
role and proving fit. It links
[[career-transitions-in-data=>career transition]],
[[career growth]],
[[CV screening]], and
[[job descriptions]]. A good
search also tests whether the company has the team, data, and hiring path to
use the candidate well.

DataTalks.Club guests define job search more narrowly than "apply to many
jobs." [[person:sarahmestiri|Sarah Mestiri]] builds it
around goals and strategy in
[[cite:job-search-strategy-in-tech-projects-skills-cv-networking|Tech Job Search Strategy]].
She also covers networking and CV work there.

[[person:lukewhipps=>Luke Whipps]] adds the recruiter
view in
[[cite:get-data-scientist-job|Land Data Scientist Roles]].
[[person:nicksingh=>Nick Singh]] treats interview
success as a communication problem in
[[cite:data-interview-behavioral-and-portfolio-prep-guide|Ace Data Interviews]].
Together, those episodes frame job search as a matching problem. Candidates
choose a role, build proof, explain that proof, and then evaluate the company.

## Targeting the Role

Role targeting starts by translating titles into work. Sarah asks candidates to
define the ideal role through tasks, skills, and future vision, then validate
that choice with role analysis and informational interviews. She separates
interest from market demand so candidates can pick a search direction before
tailoring applications
[[cite:job-search-strategy-in-tech-projects-skills-cv-networking|Tech Job Search Strategy]].

[[person:lindsaymcquade=>Lindsay McQuade]] gives the
junior-candidate version in
[[cite:get-junior-data-job-and-transferable-skills|Land Junior Data Jobs]].
The market blurs data scientist, analyst, and engineer titles, so a candidate
needs a job focus before rewriting a CV or portfolio. Her advice pairs well
with [[Data Scientist Role]],
[[Data Analyst Careers]],
and [[Data Engineer Role]]
when the title alone is too broad.

Data roles make this boundary important. [[person:olegnovikov|Oleg Novikov]]
separates product data science from machine learning engineering
[[cite:data-science-interview-and-cv-guide|Data Science Interview Guide]].
[[person:terezaiofciu=>Tereza Iofciu]] warns in
[[cite:data-science-job-red-flags-and-mismatched-roles|Data Science Jobs]]
that titles can hide the real work and team maturity. Candidates should read
[[Job Descriptions]] as evidence
about responsibilities, not as a tool wishlist.

## Search Breadth and Tailoring

Guests differ on how wide to search. [[person:jeffkatz|Jeff Katz]]
advises data engineering candidates to apply broadly and avoid self-filtering
too early
[[cite:get-data-engineering-job-prep-and-interview|Data Engineering Job Prep and Interview Guide]].
Luke and Sarah draw a narrower boundary. Luke recommends target-company lists,
while Sarah recommends segmentation and focused outreach
[[cite:get-data-scientist-job|Land Data Scientist Roles]]
[[cite:job-search-strategy-in-tech-projects-skills-cv-networking|Tech Job Search Strategy]].

The disagreement isn't between mass applications and applying to five jobs.
Early candidates may need volume to learn the market. Guests still warn that
unfocused volume weakens CV tailoring, interview preparation, and networking.

Lindsay makes the middle path explicit for juniors. She recommends enough
applications to get market signal and enough tailoring to learn from each
attempt
[[cite:get-junior-data-job-and-transferable-skills|Land Junior Data Jobs]].
That balance sits between [[career-transitions-in-data|career transition]]
and [[hiring]], because the candidate has
to learn how recruiters and teams describe fit.

For entry-level data science candidates, a smart broad search means applying
even when a posting lists some unfamiliar tools. Rejection and interview
questions then become market feedback. Katie Bauer's B2B SaaS hiring advice
adds the employer-side version. Junior candidates stand out when outreach and
interview preparation show that they understand the team, product, and role.
That moves the application away from a generic funnel
([[cite:hiring-and-managing-data-science-teams-in-b2b-saas|Hiring and Managing Data Science Teams in B2B SaaS]]).

The search should still point toward a target company or industry. Find
practitioners in that company or nearby roles. Ask for a short conversation.
Make the request specific enough to answer
quickly.[[cite:how-to-break-into-data-science|Playbook]]
That makes [[Data Science Careers]] and
[[Data Scientist CV and Portfolio]] part of the same loop. Choose a direction,
build evidence, test it with the market, then refine the next application.

## CV Evidence

Across the job-search episodes, the CV acts as a proof surface. Luke starts
with first impressions, formatting, and clarity. He asks candidates to connect
tools to projects, use cases, and business impact. He also tells them to
research job needs before mapping the CV to those needs
[[cite:get-data-scientist-job|Land Data Scientist Roles]].

Oleg gives a similar rule. The CV should be easy to scan, foreground personal
contribution, and remove irrelevant personal details. He treats the CV like a
landing page for the next hiring step rather than a biography
[[cite:data-science-interview-and-cv-guide|Data Science Interview Guide]].

For junior and transition candidates, the CV has to translate prior work.
Lindsay focuses on transferable skills and moves candidates from responsibility
lists toward achievement-based evidence
[[cite:get-junior-data-job-and-transferable-skills|Land Junior Data Jobs]].

[[person:nicolasrassam=>Nicolas Rassam]]
gives the data engineering version in
[[cite:hiring-for-data-engineering-jobs-in-europe|Hiring Data Engineers in Europe]].
Candidates should show SQL and Python. They should also show the problem
solved and the outcome. That makes [[CV Screening]]
part of job search, not only an employer-side topic.

## Portfolio Proof

Portfolio work matters when it shows reasoning through a real problem. Sarah
argues that projects validate skills better than course completion alone
[[cite:job-search-strategy-in-tech-projects-skills-cv-networking|Tech Job Search Strategy]].
Oleg opens with the same idea
[[cite:data-science-interview-and-cv-guide|Data Science Interview Guide]].
A project can differentiate an application before the interview when it shows a
candidate's own decisions.

The expected project changes by role, so Jeff recommends personal projects and
open-source contributions. Clean code, useful names, and tests are part of the
data engineering signal
[[cite:get-data-engineering-job-prep-and-interview|Data Engineering Job Prep and Interview Guide]].
Nicolas adds that standout projects should be shareable and explainable
[[cite:hiring-for-data-engineering-jobs-in-europe|Hiring Data Engineers in Europe]].

Nick asks candidates to lead with ownership and impact
[[cite:data-interview-behavioral-and-portfolio-prep-guide|Ace Data Interviews]].
Public work becomes interview material when the candidate can explain
tradeoffs, metrics, ownership, and business context. For role-specific project
shapes, see
[[Open Source Portfolio Evidence]],
[[Data Engineering Portfolio Projects]],
and
[[Machine Learning Portfolio Projects]].

## Networking and Referrals

Networking in the job-search episodes is targeted research, not mass messaging.
Sarah recommends weak ties, referrals, informational interviews, and weekly
outreach. She makes outreach short and personalized, then asks about day-to-day
work and success factors
[[cite:job-search-strategy-in-tech-projects-skills-cv-networking|Tech Job Search Strategy]].
Those questions help candidates evaluate the role while they build a referral
path.

Nick gives the interview-prep version in
[[cite:data-interview-behavioral-and-portfolio-prep-guide|Ace Data Interviews]].
Cold emails work better when they include project links and specific evidence.
Lindsay adds LinkedIn informational outreach in
[[cite:get-junior-data-job-and-transferable-skills|Land Junior Data Jobs]].
That's especially useful for juniors who can't rely on recruiters.

Tatiana Gabruseva adds the senior transition version
[[cite:from-academia-to-staff-ai-engineer-interviews-and-career-growth|From Academia to Staff AI Engineer]].
Referrals and network warmth can change which applications turn into
interviews. Early rejections become feedback on gaps to fix before the next
attempt.

Recruiter attention is easier to earn when outreach shows company research.
Before contacting a company or employee, candidates should understand the
industry and product well enough to ask interesting questions. They should also
know the likely technical challenges. A couple of relevant projects give the
conversation a concrete proof surface. Personal Streamlit apps on top of
machine learning work can be enough when they match the role.[[cite:how-to-break-into-data-science|Playbook]]

Use [[Machine Learning Portfolio Projects]] when that proof needs a clearer
project brief, baseline, evaluation, and follow-up story.

## Interviews and Assessments

Interview preparation starts with the hiring path. Oleg outlines a common data
science funnel in
[[cite:data-science-interview-and-cv-guide|Data Science Interview Guide]].
The sequence usually starts with a recruiter screen, then moves to take-home
work and later interview rounds. Jeff gives the
data engineering version in
[[cite:get-data-engineering-job-prep-and-interview|Data Engineering Job Prep and Interview Guide]],
where SQL, Python, and take-home projects are common formats. Nicolas adds that
assessment varies by level in
[[cite:hiring-for-data-engineering-jobs-in-europe|Hiring Data Engineers in Europe]].

Guests also draw different boundaries around portfolio and assessment work.
Oleg treats take-home projects as a return-on-investment decision
[[cite:data-science-interview-and-cv-guide|Data Science Interview Guide]].
Tereza warns in
[[cite:data-science-job-red-flags-and-mismatched-roles|Data Science Jobs]]
that take-home tasks can overburden candidates. Candidates should ask what the
assessment measures and how much unpaid work it requires.

The behavioral and case-interview episodes are communication heavy. Nick
explains that interviewers need more than technical skill, so he recommends grid
planning and STAR stories. For case or product-sense interviews, he starts by
clarifying goals and metrics, then handles assumptions explicitly
[[cite:data-interview-behavioral-and-portfolio-prep-guide|Ace Data Interviews]].

The same structured reasoning appears in
[[Machine Learning System Design]],
where candidates explain requirements and baselines. They also explain metrics
and tradeoffs.

## Career Changers and Juniors

Career changers need a bridge story, not an apology. Sarah works with career
changers and return-to-work candidates in
[[cite:job-search-strategy-in-tech-projects-skills-cv-networking|Tech Job Search Strategy]].
She advises candidates to lead with results and transferable skills. Lindsay
builds the junior version in
[[cite:get-junior-data-job-and-transferable-skills|Land Junior Data Jobs]]:
past experience becomes recruiter-friendly evidence for data roles.

Guests also offer proof paths without commercial experience. Jeff names
nonprofits, paid projects, and internships in
[[cite:get-data-engineering-job-prep-and-interview|Data Engineering Job Prep and Interview Guide]].
Oleg gives PhD and cold-start candidates a project, synthetic-data, and
blogging path in
[[cite:data-science-interview-and-cv-guide|Data Science Interview Guide]].

[[AI Tooling]] doesn't block starting in [[Data Science Careers]] now. It can
help beginners get started faster. Junior hiring creates the harder constraint
because companies may still prefer senior candidates.[[cite:datatalksclub-scaling-and-free-courses|Scaling Free Courses]]

The long-term [[Hiring]] pyramid still needs juniors to replace seniors who
move up or leave for other jobs and self-employment.
That pushes junior search toward targeted role choice, portfolio evidence, and
translated prior expertise instead of course completion alone.
For fuller transition routes, use
[[career-transitions-in-data=>Career Transition]],
[[Career Transitions in Data]],
and [[Academia]].

## Data Engineering Search

Data engineering search is more concrete than generic "data" search. The role
can be tested through pipelines, code, SQL, and operational judgment. Jeff
names Python and SQL, Docker and Airflow, and warehouse work. He also names code
quality and database concepts, while warning that certificates shouldn't replace
skill proof and fundamentals
[[cite:get-data-engineering-job-prep-and-interview|Data Engineering Job Prep and Interview Guide]].

Nicolas adds the recruiter view in
[[cite:hiring-for-data-engineering-jobs-in-europe|Hiring Data Engineers in Europe]].
Transferable experience from software and BI roles matters.
Junior-to-senior expectations differ. For career switchers, focused skills plus
projects matter. This candidate-side view complements
[[Data Engineer Role]],
[[data-engineer-roadmap=>Data Engineering Roadmap]],
[[Data Engineering Portfolio Projects]],
and [[Hiring]].

## Company Evaluation and Offers

Tereza gives the clearest candidate-side company checklist in
[[cite:data-science-job-red-flags-and-mismatched-roles|Data Science Jobs]].
Candidates should ask about team structure, objectives, data maturity, and
responsibilities. They should also ask about salary transparency and whether
the company knows what it wants from the role. That makes job search a
two-sided fit check, not only an application funnel.

Offer and rejection handling also matter. Oleg discusses offer components,
market comparison, negotiation, and current-salary problems in
[[cite:data-science-interview-and-cv-guide|Data Science Interview Guide]].
He recommends gracious replies to rejections because relationships can matter
later. Luke adds salary signals and recruiter trust in
[[cite:get-data-scientist-job|Land Data Scientist Roles]],
including how transparent to be about other interviews. For the compensation
side of the same conversation, see
[[Salary Negotiation]].
